I was summoned for jury duty in the county courts in March of 2026. Like most
people, I was somewhat annoyed by the obligation and the disruption in routine.
I had to report on the Monday immediately following a work trip, which made the
timing even less optimal. I'd been summoned a year prior and dismissed, and I
recall a clerk explaining that Monday and Tuesday are the worst days to report,
since longer trials often start then. Later in the week is better - less typical
to start a long trial toward the end of the week. This is what I recall, anyway.

I reported with some assumption that I'd be dismissed. Of course, the vast
majority of people are trying to avoid it. I really didn't want to do it going
in, but I also refused to be dishonest to try to get out of it. Hundreds of
candidates were whittled down into specific courts, and further narrowed to 14
people - 12 jurors, 2 backups.

Not only was I selected, but the judge explained that this trial was expected to
last two full weeks. I was already a bit stressed, and the prospect of two more
weeks of disruption was really dreadful. I also still felt a bit vulnerable in
the fitness routine I'd started the past summer, and I was worried how jury
duty would slow, delay, or even reverse my progress.

So I decided to commute by bike - at least partially. I live about 30 miles
round trip by bicycle from the courthouse, and the last 6 miles back is a bit of
a climb. Not a serious grade, but it gets a little tiresome after an already
long ride throughout the city. No way I could commit to that for several days
back-to-back. Instead, I drove my bike in my truck to a [park and
ride](https://maps.app.goo.gl/L5nJWfst3a8EXHJZ9) each morning and rode the [9.2
miles south on the Pikes Peak Greenway
Trail](https://maps.app.goo.gl/SsLE3geJLPowzxMB7) to the El Paso County
Courthouse - still over 18 miles round trip on the days I went in.

I mostly checked out from work, as you're supposed to, but I probably wouldn't
have had much left for it anyway. The trial was more exhausting than I
expected. The case involved a long list of charges, some of them serious and
difficult to sit with, and I felt the responsibility of considering them
carefully. The rides gave me time to think. I enjoyed that part of it. The
weather those couple of weeks was really starting to warm up, so it was also a
good chance to spend some time walking around downtown on lunch breaks.

I'll say - the El Paso County courts run a smooth operation. I was impressed
with the judge, the defense, and the district attorney. It wasn't a super
interesting case, but we had several days of deliberation, with lots of
evidence and witnesses. The only other jury duty I'd done was several years
ago, a single trial that lasted a single day.

Ultimately, the jury hung on a few charges (actually one dissident). That was a
mistrial on those, and they'd be retried (note to self: check the status). The
charges we agreed on were settled and would be sentenced separately.

{% photo 2026/IMG_0990.jpeg %}

One of the days, I came out and the accessories on my bike were gone - my
lights and my bag. It was irksome, but not surprising. It's wild to me that the
bike rack directly in front of the county courthouse is so susceptible to theft.

I'm surprised I didn't take more pictures those couple of weeks. The one
featured here was taken looking up outside the windows of a corridor in the
courthouse. It had a [Lynchian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lynch)
vibe, as did the entire experience and several of the personalities.

It was an odd couple of weeks in more than one way - the weight of sitting on
that jury, and an urban bike commute for someone who's worked from home for the
past several years. Those rides raised my fitness base more than I expected. I
still wouldn't volunteer for two weeks in a county courtroom, but I'm glad I
had the bike for them.
