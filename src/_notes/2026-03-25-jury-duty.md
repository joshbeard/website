I had jury duty that spanned a couple of weeks in March of 2026. I was eight or
nine months into riding, and I didn't want to put it on pause or try to fit a
ride in at the end of the day. Riding all the way from home would have added
another 12 miles round trip, and half of that is a tiresome climb back. So I
drove to a [park and ride](https://maps.app.goo.gl/L5nJWfst3a8EXHJZ9) each
morning and rode the [9.2 miles south on the Pikes Peak Greenway Trail](https://maps.app.goo.gl/SsLE3geJLPowzxMB7) to the El
Paso County Courthouse - over 18
miles on the days I went in.

I mostly checked out from work, as you're supposed to, but I probably wouldn't
have had much left for it anyway. The trial was more exhausting than I
expected. The case involved a long list of charges, some of them serious and
difficult to sit with, and I felt the responsibility of considering them
carefully. Riding gave me time to think and helped turn the disruption into a
more positive experience. I enjoyed that part of it.

{% photo 2026/IMG_0990.jpeg %}

One of the days, I came out and the accessories on my bike were gone - my
lights and my bag. It was irksome, but not surprising. It's wild to me that the
bike rack directly in front of the county courthouse is so susceptible to theft.

I'll say - the El Paso County courts run a smooth operation. Hundreds of jury
candidates, quickly and precisely whittled down. Of course, the vast majority of
people are trying to avoid it. I really didn't want to do it going in, but I
also refused to be dishonest to try to get out of it. Not only was I selected,
but it was for a case that was expected to last two full weeks. I _really_
didn't want to do that. I was impressed with the judge, the defense, and the
district attorney. It wasn't a super interesting case, but it did have several
charges - a few of them pretty heavy.  We had several days of deliberation, with
lots of evidence and witnesses. The only other jury duty I'd done was several
years ago, a single trial that lasted a single day.

The timing was inconvenient - coming off a week of work travel, and more
disruption on top of that - but I embraced it. The weather those couple of
weeks was really starting to warm up, so it was a fantastic opportunity to ride
into downtown and spend some time walking around on lunch breaks.

Ultimately, the jury hung on a few charges (actually one dissident). That was a
mistrial on those, and they'd be retried (note to self: check the status). The
charges we agreed on were settled and would be sentenced separately.

I'm surprised I didn't take more pictures those couple of weeks. The one
featured in this post was taken looking up outisde the windows of a cooridor in
the courthouse. It had a [Lynchian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lynch)
vibe, as did the entire experience and several of the personalities.

It was a unique
experience for me, an almost urban cycling commute, the pleasant parts and the
sketchy ones. Jury duty pulled me out of my usual routine for a couple of weeks.
It was a positive experience overall, despite the heavy duty of
being a juror on a case with serious accusations and difficult circumstances.
