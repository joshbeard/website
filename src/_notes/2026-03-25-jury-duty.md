I had jury duty from March 18 to March 25. I was eight or nine months into
riding, and I didn't want to put it on pause or try to fit a ride in at the end
of the day. Riding all the way from home would have added another 12 miles
round trip, and half of that is a tiresome climb back. So I drove to a
[park and ride](https://maps.app.goo.gl/L5nJWfst3a8EXHJZ9) each morning and rode
the 9.2 miles south on the Pikes Peak Greenway Trail to the [El Paso County
Courthouse](https://maps.app.goo.gl/1v9DuMRBTvZ1iJEV7) - over 18 miles on the
days I went in.

I checked out from work, as you're supposed to. I took it as an opportunity to
increase my mileage and turn a disruption into a fun experience. I enjoyed it.
The rides helped me think about the trial I was a juror on.

{% photo 2026/IMG_0990.jpeg %}

One of the days, I came out and the accessories on my bike were gone - my
lights and my bag. It was irksome, but not surprising. It's wild to me that the
bike rack directly in front of the county courthouse is so susceptible to theft.

I'll say - the El Paso County courts run a smooth operation. Hundreds of jury
candidates, quickly and precisely whittled down. I was impressed with the
judge, the defense, and the district attorney. Not a super interesting case,
but it spanned a couple of weeks and had a lot of deliberation, evidence, and
witnesses. The only other jury duty I'd done was several years ago, a single
trial that lasted a single day.

The timing was inconvenient - coming off a week of work travel, and more
disruption on top of that - but I embraced it. The weather those couple of
weeks was really starting to warm up, so it was a fantastic opportunity to ride
into downtown and spend some time walking around on lunch breaks.

Ultimately, the jury hung on a few charges (actually one dissident). That was a
mistrial on those, and they'd be retried (note to self: check the status). The
charges we agreed on were settled and would be sentenced separately.

Surprised I didn't take more pictures those couple of weeks. The one featured in
this post was taken looking up outisde the windows of a cooridor in the
courthouse. It had a [Lynchian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lynch) vibe,
as did the entire experience and several of the personalities.

It was a unique
experience for me, an almost urban cycling commute, the pleasant parts and the
sketchy ones. Serving as a juror put me next to a different lifestyle for a
couple of weeks. It was a positive experience overall, despite the heavy duty of
being a juror on a case with serious accusations and difficult circumstances.
