I've always liked the stretch of land between Denver and Colorado Springs along
I-25 - especially around [Palmer Lake and
Larkspur](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Yc6T3MTfmwMtRqDTA). Passing it on the
interstate, I also wondered whether trails that close to the highway would feel
undesirable. On August 15, 2026, I finally went out there: a 32-mile ride from
the [Baptist Road trailhead](https://maps.app.goo.gl/XZ1GXugqeQ1RjBgJ6) in
Monument north through [Palmer Lake](https://maps.app.goo.gl/9cDEtVQbkFjJ4j5N7)
and [Greenland Open Space](https://maps.app.goo.gl/MtkEoQyYkJhmpMNn8) to
[Larkspur](https://maps.app.goo.gl/DKqQ9CM6ew2a39Mx5), then back. It was one of
the more unique and memorable outings I'd done at that point.

{% photo 2026/IMG_1411.jpeg %}

I'd ridden on the [New Sante Fe
Trail](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/colorado/santa-fe-regional-trail)
through the US Air Force Academy to Baptist Road once, and short of that several
times, but I hadn't been north of Baptist Road on the trail. Unfortunately, the
trail was closed on the south end of the Air Force Academy in the spring of 2026
indefinitely, so I hadn't been able to travel through it on my usual rides. The
stretch between Monument and Palmer Lake is plenty scenic on its own, with Ben
Lomand Mountain along the way.

Greenland Open Space itself was deceptively taxing. It isn't a big mountain or a
serious climb, but there's a decent climb to get up onto the ridge. I really
enjoyed the time on top - pale rock formations, some pines, long views across
terrain that felt distinct from what I'd been riding farther south. It had a
surreal, almost liminal feel. Quiet and open. Cattle grazed the land and were
among the trails, though I wasn't near any throughout my ride. It isn't
especially remote up there - it runs along I-25 - but it still felt isolating.
I didn't encounter many people, and the scenery felt desolate in a way I liked.

{% photos 2026/IMG_1414.jpeg 2026/IMG_1423.jpeg %}

North of Greenland, the trail to Larkspur became rougher and looked like it
didn't see much traffic. I passed one couple. There was thick mud, deep ruts,
tall grass, and rocky terrain along the way - slippery in spots after a ton of
recent rain, which had also made the landscape greener. I was glad to reach the
turnaround in Larkspur. Eventually I want to continue north from there toward
Castle Rock.

{% photo 2026/IMG_1427.jpeg %}

On the way back through Greenland, I passed an area of pale rock formations that
reminded me of an uncolored version of the [Paint Mines Interpretive
Park](https://parks.elpasoco.com/parks-and-recreation/paint-mines-interpretive-park/)
east of Colorado Springs.

{% photo 2026/IMG_1454.jpeg %}

{% photo 2026/IMG_1466.jpeg %}

The rougher stretches were hard on my body, and I was thoroughly tired by the
time I got back. Still, it was nicer than I'd expected for trails that close to
the interstate. I'll definitely go back - and I think it'd be a good place to
visit in winter, maybe with snowshoes.
